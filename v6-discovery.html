Architecture
The workspace model
Up to V5, Hiviz has modules — features you build and deploy. The workspace model is a higher-order concept: bounded, configurable spaces that clients enable based on their maturity and context. Each workspace has its own objects, its own maintenance UI, its own AI layer. When multiple workspaces are enabled, they compound. The platform gets smarter with each addition — not because of more features, but because each new source of structured knowledge makes the existing sources more interpretable.

The compounding principle
Each workspace is independently valuable. But the platform's intelligence compounds at the intersections. An observation is more interpretable when the platform knows the applicable procedure. A control verification failure is more meaningful when the relevant SWMS is ingested. An investigation is more precise when contributing factors can be matched against documented requirements. The management system workspace is the knowledge layer that makes everything else smarter.

Compounding intelligence
What each combination unlocks
The value of enabling multiple workspaces isn't additive — it's multiplicative. Each new workspace provides context that makes the existing ones more powerful. Here are the key intelligence unlocks at each combination.
Workspaces combined Intelligence unlocked
Observations + Management System
AI can compare what the observation describes against what the procedure requires for that work type. The gap between documented standard and observed practice is now a structured finding — not an inference from general safety knowledge, but a specific delta against your standard. Observation enrichment gains a new output: procedure_gap_detected and the relevant requirement clause.
The question changes from "what happened?" to "what happened versus what was supposed to happen?"
Risk + Management System
A critical control can reference the procedure that governs it. A failed verification can surface the exact clause the failure relates to. The verification prompt for a control can be derived from the procedure requirement. When a defeating factor triggers, the platform can surface the relevant procedure for replacement specifications.
Controls become living references to documented standards — not stand-alone requirements.
Incidents + Management System
Investigation AI assistance is grounded in your specific standards — not general best practice. Contributing factors are matched against procedure requirements with clause references. The platform retrieves the document version in force at the incident date. The gap between "what the procedure required" and "what was done" becomes a structured finding with specific attribution. FW Map classification confidence increases significantly.
The most legally significant connection. A version-controlled document in force at the time of an incident, with investigation findings linked to specific requirements, is the most defensible investigation record available.
Risk + Incidents + Management System
Full triangulation. An investigation identifying a control failure references the governing procedure. The procedure specifies what the control should be. The control record shows its verification history. The platform can surface: this control failed, it was verified as present 18 hours before the incident, the governing procedure requires re-verification after the specific triggering event that occurred. That's a complete evidential picture.
The platform becomes a system of record — not just for events, but for the standards that were supposed to prevent them and the evidence trail of whether they were in place.
All four workspaces
The platform has a complete model: what was supposed to happen (management system), what controls were supposed to prevent harm (risk), what people observed in the field (observations), what went wrong when it did (incidents). The Forge Works Map® classification becomes grounded, precise, and longitudinally trackable. The diagnostic loop is complete.
The workspace itself
Management System workspace
The Management System workspace has two primary purposes: authoring and serving. Authoring is the work of maintaining the documented standard — creating, reviewing, versioning, approving documents. Serving is delivering the right document to the right person at the right moment based on what's happening at their site. The AI ingestion layer is what makes serving intelligent — the platform doesn't just retrieve documents, it understands them.
Authoring
Create and maintain
Upload or author documents directly. Rich text editor for procedure authoring. PDF upload for existing documents. Version control with approval workflow. Regional variation management. Supersession tracking.
Serving
Deliver contextually
Documents pushed to worksites by work type — same governance as controls. Surfaced at the moment they're relevant: before a job starts, in a visit briefing pack, during an investigation. Not a library you search — documents that come to you.
Understanding
AI ingestion layer
The platform ingests each document and extracts structured requirements. Now it knows what a PTW must contain, what a confined space entry requires, what the pre-start checklist should verify. This knowledge becomes context for every AI call in the platform.

Data model
The document entity
A document in the management system is not just a file. It carries its applicability context, its version history, its AI-extracted requirements, and its relationship to controls, observations, and incidents. Version control is not optional — the platform must know which version was in force at any given date, particularly for investigations.

AI layer
The ingestion pipeline
Document ingestion follows the same async job pattern as observation enrichment. A document is uploaded and immediately available for serving. The AI ingestion job runs in the background, extracting structured requirements. Once complete, the document's knowledge is available to every AI call in the platform. The job is idempotent and re-runs on each new version.
01
Document uploaded
PDF or authored content stored in S3/R2. Document record created. WorksiteDocument records pushed to relevant sites. Document immediately available for serving.
ai_ingested: false
02
Ingestion queued
document.ingest BullMQ job created. Same queue infrastructure as observation.enrich. Non-blocking — document is usable before ingestion completes.
job: document.ingest
03
AI extraction
AI reads document and extracts: summary, document type confirmation, work type signals, key requirements with clause references, condition triggers, obligation levels (shall/must/should).
claude-sonnet-4-6
04
Requirements stored
DocumentRequirement records created — each a structured, queryable requirement with clause reference, condition, and verification signal. Linked to the version, not the document, for point-in-time queries.
ai_ingested: true
05
Knowledge available
All AI prompts in the platform can now query requirements for the work type. Observation enrichment, insight generation, investigation assistance, and talk generation all benefit immediately.
context: queryable

With the management system workspace enabled, every AI call in the platform gains context it didn't have before. The delta isn't subtle — it's the difference between inference from general knowledge and grounded analysis of your specific standards.

System health
Document atrophy
The management system has its own atrophy signal — the degree to which the documented standard is drifting out of currency, relevance, or coverage. A management system with overdue reviews, superseded documents not yet replaced, and low field access rates is quietly becoming unreliable. The platform surfaces this before something forces it to become obvious.
Document atrophy score
score =
(days_past_review_due × 2.0) +
(superseded_not_replaced × 12) +
(incidents_since_last_review × 8) +
(observations_linked_not_actioned × 3)
A procedure last reviewed 18 months ago with 3 linked near-misses since and 2 superseded requirements not updated scores critically. The field access rate provides a different signal — a procedure nobody reads may be irrelevant or inaccessible.
Passive signals the clockwork reveals
A procedure with zero field accesses in 90 days while its work type is active at 8 sites — either not reaching supervisors or being ignored
Observation pattern diverging from a procedure requirement — AI-detected gap accumulating without a procedure update
Review enquiry dispatched, response rate below 50% — the people closest to the work aren't engaging with the documented standard
Incident occurs in a work type with a procedure overdue for review — the most critical signal. The platform surfaces this immediately in the investigation context.

Open questions
Discovery questions for expert consultation
Six questions that need answers from people who manage safety management systems in practice before Phase 1 design begins.
Q1
Authoring in-platform vs upload-only — where does the work happen?
Target organisations likely already have procedures in Word, PDF, or a separate document management system. Phase 1 could be upload-only (simpler, integrates with what exists) or include in-platform authoring (more tightly controlled, better for AI ingestion, but requires migration effort).
A
Upload-only Phase 1. Accept existing formats. AI ingests from PDF. Authors maintain documents in their existing tools, upload new versions here. Lower adoption friction, works immediately.
B
In-platform authoring as the primary path, with upload as a legacy import option. Platform becomes the system of record. Higher adoption investment but better AI ingestion quality and tighter version control.
C
Start with upload, build authoring as a Phase 2 feature once clients are using the serving and review capabilities. Let adoption demonstrate value before asking for migration effort.
Do target organisations have dedicated document controllers or does the safety manager own both authoring and system maintenance? That answer shapes which phase to invest in first.
Q2
Review workflow — how formal does the approval chain need to be?
A new document version needs to be approved before it becomes active. Some organisations have a single approver (the safety manager). Others have a multi-stage approval chain — technical review, legal review, management sign-off. The platform needs to support the workflow without over-engineering it for simpler contexts.
A
Single approver — one nominated person per document type approves and publishes. Simple, fast, works for most construction and industrial contexts.
B
Configurable approval chain — define 1–3 approvers per document type. Each must sign off in sequence before the version becomes active. More overhead but mirrors how some regulated industries work.
C
Simple approval with an optional consultation step — one approver, but they can dispatch a review enquiry to field users before approving. Gets field input without requiring a formal chain.
Most construction and industrial organisations use Option A or C in practice, even if their formal procedures describe B. Build A + C first.
Q3
Field access model — how do supervisors read documents on site?
A supervisor about to start a hot work job needs to be able to access the PTW procedure and the relevant SWMS. But reading a 12-page procedure on a phone is not viable. The platform needs an access model that works in the field — probably AI-summarised key points, with the full document available if needed.
A
AI-generated key points only — a 3–5 point summary of the most critical requirements for this work type, surfaced before work starts. Full document accessible via a tap but not the primary interface.
B
Full document as a mobile-optimised reader — formatted for phone, searchable, with the current section for the job pre-highlighted. More complete but more friction.
C
AI-summarised key points in context — not a separate document view but integrated into the work context. Pre-start checklist prompted by procedure requirements. Observation capture prompts relevant procedure clauses when a gap is detected.
Option C is the most consistent with the KISS principle — documents come to supervisors in context rather than supervisors going to documents. Requires Phase 3 AI ingestion to work properly.
Q4
Document review via enquiry — consent and framing with the workforce?
Using the enquiry tool to review a procedure is operationally elegant — it reuses existing infrastructure, generates an auditable record, and involves the people closest to the work. But supervisors receiving an enquiry asking "does this procedure reflect what actually happens?" need to feel safe answering honestly. This is adjacent to the V4 diagnostic survey anonymisation question.
A
Named responses — supervisor's identity is visible to the safety manager. Consistent with how the rest of the enquiry system works. Relies on organisational trust that honest feedback is valued, not punished.
B
Anonymous responses for procedure review enquiries specifically — same hard anonymisation as V4 diagnostic surveys. Gets more honest answers about whether procedures reflect reality. Different data layer rule than standard enquiries.
C
Configurable per review enquiry — safety manager decides when to use anonymous vs named based on the sensitivity of the procedure being reviewed.
Procedure review is one of the places where anonymous responses are likely to be dramatically more useful. A supervisor saying "this procedure doesn't match how we actually do the job" needs to feel safe doing so.
Q5
Regulatory document integration — pull from regulator sources or manual upload?
Regulatory requirements (WHS codes of practice, industry standards, Safe Work Australia guidance) change. A management system that includes regulatory documents needs a way to keep them current. Manual upload works but creates maintenance overhead. API integration with regulatory sources is complex but keeps documents automatically current.
A
Manual upload only — organisation maintains its own set of regulatory documents. Platform notifies when a regulatory document approaches a review date. Simple, no dependency on external APIs.
B
Curated regulatory library — Forge Works maintains a library of key regulatory documents (Safe Work Australia, state WHS codes) that clients can link to their document set. Forge Works handles currency. Commercially differentiated.
C
Start with A (manual), design the data model to support B later. If the commercial opportunity is there, build the curated library as a premium feature in a later phase.
Option B is potentially a strong commercial differentiator — organisations pay for the certainty that regulatory documents are current. Significant Forge Works curation effort but reusable across all clients in the same jurisdiction.
Q6
AI ingestion quality — what level of extraction confidence is acceptable?
AI extraction of requirements from a procedure is powerful but not perfect. A poorly structured PDF, ambiguous clause language, or complex conditional logic can produce inaccurate requirements. Using those requirements to ground investigation contributing factors creates a liability question: what if the AI extracted a requirement incorrectly and that extraction influenced an investigation finding?
A
All AI-ingested requirements are clearly labelled as "AI-extracted — confirm against source document." Users always have access to the source document. Requirements are context, not authoritative findings.
B
Human review step for extracted requirements before they become active — safety manager reviews and confirms (or corrects) the AI extraction before it's used in AI calls. More overhead but defensible.
C
Confidence-gated extraction — same 0.70 threshold as FW Map classification. Requirements below threshold flagged for review before use. High-confidence requirements used immediately. Partial automation with targeted human review.
Option C is most consistent with the platform's existing AI output principle — every AI output is a suggestion with visible reasoning. Requirements are suggestions until human-confirmed. The source document is always the authority. This is also the safest legal position.
Roadmap
Placement and sequencing
The management system workspace spans V3 through V6 across three phases. The workspace model itself — the configurable enabling/disabling of workspaces, the worksite layer system, the compound intelligence connections — should be designed as a platform-level architecture decision at V3 build time, even if only one or two workspaces are enabled in early versions.

The architecture decision to make at V3
The workspace model — how workspaces are enabled per client, how layers appear on the worksite dashboard, how workspace connections are plumbed — should be designed as a platform-level decision at V3. Not because V3 needs all four workspaces, but because retrofitting a workspace architecture onto a platform that wasn't designed for it is significantly more expensive than building it in from V3.
→A workspace_config table at org level — which workspaces are enabled, which are licensed, feature flags per workspace
→Worksite dashboard layer system — each workspace registers its layer components, only enabled layers render
→Workspace connection hooks — observation enrichment checks if Management System is enabled before querying DocumentRequirements; investigation assistance checks if both Incidents and Management System are enabled
→Sidebar navigation adapts to enabled workspaces — Risk and Management System sections only visible when enabled
The closing thought
Every piece of infrastructure built from V1 through V5 is now leveraged by the management system workspace. The push/override model, the enquiry tool, the AI job queue, the async enrichment pattern, the worksite anchor, the version control concepts from the control register — it all becomes the plumbing for serving and understanding documented standards. The product doesn't need new capabilities to deliver V6. It needs the knowledge layer that makes all the existing capabilities dramatically smarter. That's the platform.
