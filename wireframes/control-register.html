The bowtie
made manageable
Work type → hazard → prevention controls → mitigation controls. Lifecycle, defeating factors, push/accept. Five screens showing how this could work.
Design considerations captured here: Prevention controls (left of bowtie — stop it happening) are visually distinguished from mitigation controls (right of bowtie — limit consequences). The hazard/unwanted event sits at the centre. Lifecycle states: implementing → active → not required → superseded. Defeating factors: time-limited conditions that erode a control's effectiveness (UV degradation of a barrier, rated anchor approaching service life) — these demand scheduled clockwork, not just one-off verification. Site distribution bar gives instant health signal per control. Push/accept model lets global propagate with local accountability.

Defeating factors — MVP note
Not required in MVP — but the schema needs to carry the concept from the start.

A defeating factor is a condition that erodes a control's effectiveness over time or under specific circumstances: anchor rated life, UV degradation, calibration drift, filter saturation.

The clockwork becomes meaningfully different when it tracks whether the control is effective — not just whether it is present.
