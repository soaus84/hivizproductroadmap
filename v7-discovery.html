Foundation
Why native? Why now?
Every design decision in the Communities workspace flows from a single product philosophy. Get this wrong and CoP becomes another chat tool. Get it right and it becomes the connective tissue of a learning organisation.
"Slack is for what people want for lunch on Friday.
Hiviz is operationally significant content only."
The Communities workspace is native to Hiviz — not an integration, not an adapter, not a posting mechanism that pipes content to an external platform. It lives inside the same product where supervisors log observations, managers plan visits, and safety managers review insights. The conversation happens in the same context as the work. That's the distinction that matters.

Communities is also not an obligation system. It sits adjacent to the duty list — the toolbox talks, the enquiries, the corrective actions. Duty is what you must do. CoP is where you go when you want to learn, contribute, or understand more. One reinforces the other without conflating them.
⚡
The productive irony
The Forge Works Map® factor communications_coordination describes how well information flows and how coordinated teams are across a site and organisation. It's one of the most frequently underperforming factors in field intelligence. The Communities workspace is, quite literally, a vehicle for improving that factor — a platform designed to surface coordination failures is itself an instrument of better coordination. That connection should be explicit in how CoP is positioned and measured.
Architecture
Four community classes
Four classes of community, each with different membership rules, scope logic, and purpose. All four coexist and interact. Users may belong to communities across multiple classes simultaneously.
🏢
Organisational
Scoped to the hierarchy
Communities that follow the org structure — company-wide, divisional, regional. Vertical and authority-aware. A company-wide systemic insight about senior leadership behaviour gets discussed here. A division manager posting here reaches their division.
Membership
Automatic — derived from role and worksite hierarchy. A regional safety manager is a member of their region and all parent organisational communities.
Scope
Bounded by the org unit. Content in a divisional community is visible to that division and above, not below unless explicitly shared.
Primary use
Systemic insights, leadership-level patterns, company-wide initiatives, cross-division coordination. Not operational detail.
🎯
Practice
Scoped to safety practice
Communities that follow the safety practice taxonomy — PTW, confined space entry, traffic management. Cross-divisional and flat. A safety professional anywhere in the organisation who works in confined space entry sees the same community as their peers in every region. No walls.
Membership
Automatic affiliation via practice relevance at the user's worksite. A global confined space expert is affiliated regardless of region. Subscription controls notification preferences.
Scope
Flat — cross-divisional, cross-regional, global. The practice community is the same community for everyone. Silo-free by architecture.
Primary use
Practice-specific learning, procedure gaps, shared field experience, investigation learnings relevant to the practice.
⚙️
Work type
Scoped to high-risk work taxonomy
Communities that follow the work type taxonomy — hot work, heavy vehicle operations, work at height, blasting. Cross-divisional and flat. The same logic as practice communities, but rooted in the work being done rather than the safety function managing it.
Membership
Automatic affiliation based on active work types at the user's worksite. A hot work supervisor in Region A and Region C are in the same Hot Work community.
Scope
Flat — cross-divisional, cross-regional. A regional manager's insight about hot work is visible to the entire hot work community globally.
Primary use
Work-type-specific field experience, critical control discussions, incident investigation learnings seeded from the intelligence pipeline.
🔬
Working group
Time-bounded, purpose-specific
Communities stood up around a specific problem or objective, with a defined lifecycle and an expected output. Not a permanent community — a focused, working space. When the output is produced the working group closes and its record is preserved, not deleted.
Membership
Invite-only. Created by safety manager or above. Members join by invitation, not affiliation. Membership can span roles, regions, and divisions deliberately.
Scope
Defined at creation — a working group can be local (one site) or global. Scope is intentional, not automatic. The creator decides who needs to be in the room.
Primary use
Procedure reviews, post-incident collaborative analysis, standard development, cross-regional coordination on a specific problem. Produces outputs that re-enter the platform.
Access model
Affiliation vs subscription
Two distinct concepts that are often conflated. Affiliation is automatic — it determines what communities a user belongs to based on their context. Subscription is user-controlled — it determines how and when the community reaches them.
Affiliation Automatic
Derived automatically from the user's role, worksite, active work types, and safety practices. Affiliation determines which communities are available to a user — what they can see and participate in. It cannot be manually removed, only updated when the underlying context changes.
Organisational: A regional safety manager is affiliated with their region and all parent org communities automatically on account creation.
Work type: A supervisor at a site where hot work and WAH are active is affiliated with both the Hot Work and Work at Height communities — with no manual setup required.
Practice: A safety professional whose role includes PTW is affiliated with the PTW community globally, regardless of which region they're in.
Subscription User-controlled
User-managed preferences within their affiliated communities. Subscription controls notification frequency, digest delivery, and what appears in the user's CoP feed. A supervisor might be affiliated with five work type communities but only subscribe to active notifications from two. Affiliation doesn't create noise — subscription does.
Active subscription: Immediate notification on new discussions, appears prominently in CoP feed. Appropriate for communities where the user actively contributes.
Digest subscription: Weekly summary of top discussions. Appropriate for communities relevant but not primary to the user's day-to-day.
Affiliated but unsubscribed: Community is accessible, user can participate, but no proactive notifications. Nothing is missed — it's there when they go looking.
Content model
Three discussion types
Not every discussion starts the same way. The platform generates some from workflow events; practitioners initiate others directly. The type determines how AI frames the discussion and which community it targets.
AI-Generated
Workflow-triggered discussions
When a workflow event occurs — a critical insight is approved, an investigation closes, an enquiry returns a significant finding, an atrophy pattern emerges — the platform generates a discussion seeded to the relevant community. The safety manager reviews and approves before it appears. The key design constraint: AI doesn't summarise the event and ask for comment. It reframes the event as the best possible prompt for meaningful practitioner discussion. See AI framing principles below.
Example — critical insight about spotter management in hot work
Not: "A critical insight has been identified about spotter positioning in hot work contexts. What do you think?"

But: "Spotter handovers are breaking down at shift changes — three near-misses in 28 days say the same thing across different sites. What actually happens at your site when the spotter changes over? Is it covered in your PTW?"
The opening question is the most important element — it must be specific enough that an experienced practitioner finds it worth answering. The test: would a supervisor with 15 years experience read this and think "actually, I have something to say here"?
Practitioner
Practitioner-initiated discussions
Any affiliated user can start a discussion in a community they belong to. A supervisor who has seen something in the field. A regional manager with a cross-site question. A safety professional who wants field perspectives on a proposed change. Practitioner-initiated discussions are unfiltered — they enter the community directly, without AI generation or safety manager review. Moderation happens reactively, not proactively.
Example — supervisor in Hot Work community
"We changed our fire watch positioning last month after a near-miss — watcher was too close to the work and couldn't see both sides. Has anyone else moved to a split-watch setup for confined-area hot work? How do you manage the coverage overlap?"
Practitioner-initiated discussions that generate high engagement or surface a previously undetected pattern can be elevated into the intelligence pipeline — with safety manager review as the gate. The community is a signal source, not just a destination.
Cross-posted
Cross-posted discussions
A discussion that lives in one community but is also visible in another. Cross-posting is how organisational and work/practice communities stay connected without becoming the same thing. A regional manager's discussion about hot work stays in their regional community for local context — and optionally reaches the global Hot Work community for broader input. Same thread. Same responses. No duplication of effort. See Cross-posting mechanics below.
Example — regional discussion elevated to work type community
Before posting: "Hot work is currently active across 4 other regions. Would you like to also post this to the Hot Work community? Your regional context will be preserved — it will appear as a regional cross-post."
Cross-posted discussions show their origin community clearly. Responses from both communities feed the same thread. The Hot Work community sees where the discussion came from. The regional community sees that it has reached beyond its boundaries.
Design principle
AI framing, not AI announcing
The quality of a discussion is almost entirely determined by the quality of the opening question. The AI's job is not to summarise what happened — practitioners can read the source. Its job is to reframe the event as the most meaningful prompt possible for the specific audience receiving it.
⚖️ What good framing looks like — compared
❌ Announcement framing
A Critical Insight has been generated regarding spotter management in hot work operations following 3 near-miss observations across 4 sites in the past 28 days. This insight has been approved for sharing. Please review and share your thoughts or experiences.
✓ Discussion framing
Spotter handovers are breaking down at shift changes — the same breakdown, three times in four weeks, across sites that don't share a crew. When does your PTW actually confirm the spotter knows their boundaries, and what happens to that confirmation when the spotter walks off?
❌ Generic prompt
An investigation into a lost-time injury at Site X has been closed and cleared for sharing. The root cause was identified as inadequate hot work PTW process. What are your experiences with PTW for hot work at your sites?
✓ Specific tension
A site's fire watch was positioned for compliance, not coverage — close enough to sign the PTW, too far to see both sides of the work face. What does your PTW actually say about fire watch positioning, and does anyone check it's followed rather than just completed?
❌ Atrophy announcement
This confined space entry procedure has not been reviewed in 18 months. Three near-misses related to this procedure have been logged in this period. How could this procedure be improved?
✓ Practitioner context
This procedure hasn't changed in 18 months but the work around it has — three near-misses say there's a gap between what it describes and what's actually happening at the work face. What does this procedure get wrong about how confined space entry actually runs on your sites?
The AI job cop_discussion.generate receives the workflow event plus the community context — who these people are, what they know, what work they do. The prompt instructs the model to act as a practitioner asking another practitioner a question they'd genuinely want to answer. The output is a thread_body (3–5 sentences of context) and an opening_question (one specific, tension-surfacing question). The question is the deliverable. The body is support material.

Cross-posting is always optional and always prompted, never automatic. The creator sees who will additionally receive the discussion before confirming.
A cross-posted discussion shows its origin community clearly — "from North Region Community" — so the broader audience understands the local context.
Responses from both communities feed the same thread. A Hot Work practitioner in South Region responding to a cross-posted discussion appears alongside the original regional responses. No bifurcated conversations.
The platform checks active work types and practices before suggesting cross-post targets. Only relevant communities are suggested — not all communities.
Elevated reach: if a cross-posted discussion in a work type community generates high cross-regional engagement, the platform can suggest elevating it to an organisational community with safety manager approval.
Focused work
Working groups — time-bounded, output-oriented
A fourth community class for focused collaborative work. Working groups are not permanent. They are stood up around a problem or objective, do the work, produce an output, and close. The record is preserved — the space is not.
🎯
Purpose and objective
Every working group is created with a stated purpose. Not just a name — a clear objective, a reason for existing, and an expected output. This metadata is visible to all members and to safety managers. It answers: why does this group exist, what will it produce, and when should it be done?
Required on creation
Objective: Plain-language statement of what the group will achieve
Expected output: What the group will produce — procedure amendment, recommendation, report, register change
Target close date: When the group expects to produce its output
Output destination: Where the output goes — MS workspace, control register, community post, insight pipeline
👥
Membership and scope
Invite-only. The creator decides who needs to be in the room. A working group can span roles, regions, and divisions deliberately — a confined space working group might include a supervisor from each region, a safety professional, and an external expert. No automatic affiliation applies. Members join by invitation only.
Membership properties
Creator: safety manager or above
Members: any Hiviz user, any role, any region
Visibility: members + the creator's manager level
Observers: safety managers can observe without participating
📋
Lifecycle
Working groups have a clear lifecycle. They are not open-ended. When the output is produced, the group closes — the record is preserved and the output is promoted to wherever it belongs in the platform. A working group that produces a procedure amendment submits it to the Management System workspace for review. A working group that produces a recommended control change submits it to the control register governance workflow.
Status lifecycle
active: Working group is open, members can post and contribute
output_pending: Work is done, output awaiting formal promotion
closed: Output promoted, group archived and read-only
extended: Target date passed, explicitly extended with reason
🔗
Outputs and re-entry
A working group's output doesn't disappear into a thread — it re-enters the platform. The connection between CoP and the rest of Hiviz runs through the working group output pipeline. This is how communities produce change, not just commentary.
Output destinations
Procedure amendment → Management System workspace (Document review)
Control recommendation → Risk Assurance register governance
Field insight → Intelligence pipeline (Critical Insight candidate)
Community post → Published to practice or work community on close

Platform connections
The reciprocal intelligence loop
CoP is not a one-way broadcast channel. The intelligence pipeline feeds the community — and the community feeds back into the pipeline. The platform doesn't just distribute learning; it listens for it.
Intelligence flow — inbound and outbound
Platform → CoP
Critical Insight approved → AI-generated discussion seeded to relevant practice or work type community. Safety manager reviews before posting.
Investigation closed and cleared → AI-generated discussion seeded to relevant work type community, anonymised, with investigation outcome as framing context.
Enquiry results completed → AI-generated discussion seeded with synthesis findings as framing. "The enquiry found X — does this match what you're seeing?"
Document atrophy threshold crossed → AI-generated discussion seeded to relevant practice community: "This procedure hasn't changed but the work around it has — how could it be improved?"
Company-wide systemic insight → AI-generated discussion seeded to relevant organisational community at the approved scope level.
CoP → Platform
High-engagement discussion → Surfaced to safety manager as a potential Critical Insight candidate. Safety manager reviews — if confirmed, enters intelligence pipeline with trigger_source: cop_discussion.
Practitioner-initiated discussion with cross-regional reach → Prompted for elevation to broader organisational community with safety manager approval.
Working group output → Promoted directly to destination (MS workspace, control register, insight pipeline) on group close.
Community response patterns → Aggregated engagement data contributes to CoP health signals and, over time, to the FW Map communications_coordination factor signal.
Platform metrics
CoP health signals
Community engagement is not just a usage metric — it's a signal about the organisation's learning capacity. These signals feed the analytics layer and, at sufficient volume, contribute to FW Map factor positions.
💬
Thread engagement rate
Of AI-generated and practitioner-initiated discussions, what proportion receive at least one substantive response within 7 days? Measures whether the platform is generating content worth engaging with.
responses_with_substance / total_discussions
✍️
Practitioner contribution rate
What proportion of discussions are practitioner-initiated vs AI-generated? A community dominated entirely by AI-seeded discussions is a broadcast channel. The goal is active practitioner participation, not just AI output delivery.
practitioner_initiated / total_discussions
🌍
Cross-community reach
How many discussions in work type and practice communities draw responses from practitioners across multiple regions or divisions? Measures the platform's effectiveness at breaking down silos — a direct signal for communications_coordination.
cross_region_discussions / total_work_practice_discussions
🔬
Working group completion rate
Of working groups created, what proportion close with an output promoted to the platform within their target date? Measures whether the working group mechanism is producing change or just activity.
closed_with_output_on_time / total_closed
⬆️
Community-to-pipeline rate
How often does a community discussion surface as a Critical Insight candidate that the safety manager confirms into the pipeline? Measures whether CoP is genuinely contributing to the intelligence loop, not just consuming from it.
confirmed_insight_candidates / total_surfaced

FW Map contribution
At sufficient volume, cross-community reach and engagement patterns contribute to the communications_coordination and learning_development factor signals in the FW Map capacity profile — sourced from CoP behaviour, not just field observation.

Open questions
Discovery questions
Questions to work through with product and safety experts before V7 is designed. Not decisions — discussion starters.
01
Moderation model — who owns community quality?
AI-generated discussions are reviewed before posting. Practitioner-initiated discussions are not. Over time, a CoP with hundreds of active practitioners across multiple organisations will produce content that needs moderation — not just spam, but off-topic posts, inaccurate information, or discussions that shouldn't be public at the scope they were posted to.
A
Safety managers as community moderators — they already own the intelligence gate and have the most contextual authority. Adds responsibility but aligns with their existing role.
B
Designated community stewards — a separate role, potentially a respected practitioner rather than a manager. Less hierarchical, more community-native. Could be self-nominated or appointed.
C
AI-flagged, human-confirmed — AI monitors for off-topic, potentially sensitive, or scope-inappropriate content and flags for human review. No proactive moderation role required.
D
Reactive only — any member can flag a post, flagged posts are reviewed by the community creator or safety manager. Moderation is event-driven, not role-based.
Consult: What is the expected volume of practitioner-initiated discussions per week at a mid-sized client (e.g. 500 supervisors, 20 sites)? Moderation model depends heavily on volume.
02
Cross-org communities — the long-term question
If Hiviz runs across multiple independent organisations, the work type and practice communities become very interesting. A hot work community could contain practitioners from a construction contractor, a mining company, and an infrastructure operator — all contributing to the same discussion. Insights anonymised, organisations unnamed, field experience genuinely pooled across the industry.
A
Opt-in cross-org communities — organisations choose to participate in shared work type or practice communities. Their practitioners appear as "[Organisation type] practitioner" not by name or company. Forge Works curates the cross-org layer.
B
Forge Works-facilitated communities — Forge Works hosts cross-org communities entirely outside individual org workspaces. Hiviz users can be invited to participate but it's a distinct layer.
C
Not in scope — focus on single-org value first. Cross-org communities are a commercial and architectural decision that requires a separate roadmap conversation.
Consult: This is a significant commercial and data governance decision. Cross-org communities are a potential network effect moat — the platform becomes more valuable the more organisations use it. But data sensitivity in safety intelligence is high. Not for V7 scoping, but the architecture shouldn't preclude it.
03
Practitioner-initiated discussions — approval gate or not?
AI-generated discussions require safety manager review before posting. Practitioner-initiated discussions, if also gated, remove the spontaneity that makes practitioner CoP valuable. But an ungated community in a safety context can surface sensitive content — ongoing investigations, disputed incidents, regulatory commentary.
A
No gate for practitioner posts — fully open within affiliated communities. Moderation is reactive. The community is trusted to be professional because it's operationally significant content only and the audience is curated by affiliation.
B
Soft gate — AI scans before posting, flags potential issues (mentions of named individuals, reference to ongoing investigations, regulatory content) for rapid safety manager review. Non-flagged posts go live immediately.
C
Community-class gate — organisational communities require review (higher stakes), practice and work communities are open (practitioners talking to practitioners). Different rules for different contexts.
Consult: What is the legal exposure if a practitioner posts about an ongoing investigation in a community that another org entity can see? The answer shapes the moderation model significantly.
04
Working groups — who can create them?
The current design limits working group creation to safety managers or above. This reflects the working group's connection to platform outputs — procedure amendments, control register changes, insight pipeline entries. But restricting creation too narrowly could prevent valuable practitioner-led working groups from forming.
A
Safety manager or above only — maintains the connection to platform governance. Working groups produce outputs that re-enter formal systems, so formal ownership is appropriate.
B
Any user can propose a working group — safety manager approves before it opens. Surfaces practitioner-driven need without bypassing oversight.
C
Any user can create a working group with community output only — no platform integration. Working groups that want to submit to the MS workspace or control register require safety manager sponsorship to unlock that pathway.
Consult: Are there cases where supervisors or field practitioners would benefit from their own working groups, independent of safety manager initiation? Think post-incident crew-level reviews, pre-shutdown coordination groups, or cross-site knowledge transfer for a specific job type.
05
CoP and the Hiviz duty list — how close is too close?
The platform philosophy is clear: CoP is contextual and voluntary, the duty list is obligatory. But workflow-generated discussions blur this slightly — they land in CoP as a result of actions the safety manager took in the duty system. There's a risk users begin to feel the CoP is another obligation layer rather than a genuinely open space.
A
CoP notifications are always separate from duty notifications — different notification channel, different visual treatment, clearly never a compliance item. The UI enforces the distinction.
B
Contribution to CoP is never tracked, reported on, or surfaced as a metric per individual — only aggregate community health is visible, not individual participation rates. Engagement cannot become a management target.
C
Both A and B are design constraints, not just preferences — built into the data model. No individual CoP participation data is surfaced to managers. This is architectural, not just a UI convention.
Consult: This is a cultural and trust question as much as a design question. If practitioners feel their CoP participation is being monitored by management, the quality of discussion will drop immediately. The platform's value depends on practitioners being genuinely willing to share what they know and have experienced.
Roadmap
Where Communities fits
Communities is designated V7 — the last of the named workspaces. But the foundational elements (community data model, affiliation logic, discussion infrastructure) have low dependencies and could be accelerated if commercial validation supports it. The AI framing work connects directly to the existing prompt library and is additive, not disruptive.

Communities
Native CoP workspace. Four community classes. Affiliation model. AI-framed discussions. Working groups. Reciprocal intelligence loop.
The platform can be brought forward. The community data model has low dependencies.
The affiliation model (work type, practice, org hierarchy) is derived from data that already exists in V1. The discussion infrastructure (community, discussion, response) is a new set of tables but does not require any other workspace to be live first. The AI framing work connects to the existing cop_thread.generate job already specced in the AI prompt library (Prompt 11). The only hard dependency is having meaningful workflow events to seed from — which means at least V1 needs to be live and generating insights before community discussions have something to say.

Working groups, cross-posting, and the reciprocal intelligence loop can follow as V7 depth. The community infrastructure itself could ship as early as V3 if commercial validation supports it — giving the platform a social layer at the point where enterprise customers are being acquired and team-level adoption is the conversion challenge.
